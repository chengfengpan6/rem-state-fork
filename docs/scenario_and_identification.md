# Shared interpretation with a common factual record

Scientific design revision, 25 October 2026. This document states the organizational question, the justification for the task, and recommended analysis commitments. It distinguishes those commitments from the execution manifest: a described design is not evidence that it has run. The small current engineering check and a future organizational study have different evidential purposes.

## 1. The organizational question

**When a common factual record remains available, does an inherited, collectively accessible interpretation of which sources are reliable in which contexts change subsequent evidence use and collective judgment?**

Organizations retain both records of events and interpretations of those events. A source directory, handover assessment, or recurrent judgment about whose evidence is useful may summarize observations without adding new observations. Its informational redundancy does not make its behavioral consequences trivial: members may consult, reconstruct, accept, reject, or reuse that interpretation differently. The present task isolates this distinction. It does not claim to reproduce a consulting firm, hospital, investment committee, or other field setting.

The organizational motivation draws on the distinction between information distribution, interpretation, and memory [R1–R3]; the distinction between actual expertise, attributed expertise, and influence [R4–R6]; and research on the contingent value of knowledge use and aggregation structures [R7–R8]. These literatures motivate the question. They do not establish that the implemented actors possess human expertise, social relationships, or a complete transactive memory system.

The proposed methodological contribution is a **matched intervention on an explicit shared representation while preserving its source observations**. In ordinary organizational comparisons, membership, experience, records, interpretations, and subsequent information often change together. Here the researcher can hold the event tape fixed, replace a particular representation, and observe the resulting trajectory. The resulting evidence concerns the specified synthetic organization and model configuration. It is not an estimate of an average effect in human organizations.

The substantive opportunity is to distinguish three propositions that are often conflated:

1. A common history produces an inaccurate interpretation.
2. That interpretation affects subsequent beliefs or evidence use despite access to the original record.
3. Those changes improve or impair judgment.

No single performance contrast establishes all three. A planted map can establish responsiveness to assigned content without showing how a team naturally acquired that content. An inaccurate natural map can exist without changing decisions. An effect on decisions can arise from attention or numerical copying without establishing a uniquely social mechanism.

## 2. What the construct includes

REM is the historical project abbreviation for **relational expertise memory**. Its current operational object is narrower: a context-by-source reliability map containing nine estimates and uncertainty limits. It describes the accuracy of information channels. It does not directly describe a language model's general ability, occupational competence, authority, or motives.

Three sources and three actors must be kept distinct. A source generates an externally controlled signal. An actor interprets signals and communicates. The actor initially assigned source A is not intrinsically an expert in context X; A's signal channel is simply more reliable in X under the generator. Subsequent actor forecasts can have different accuracies even when source reliabilities are fixed.

The map is a restricted analogue of an expertise directory. Full transactive memory additionally involves a division of cognitive labor and processes of encoding, storage, retrieval, and communication [R5]. Common access to a map does not prove common endorsement. In Brandon and Hollingshead's terminology, accuracy, sharedness, and validation are distinct; validation concerns participation in the system, not factual correctness [R5]. Accordingly, this project describes correction of reliability beliefs rather than treating all failures to correct as failures of TMS validation.

Deleting or replacing the map is a selective intervention on one carrier of interpretation. Retained event records, forecasts, scores, and actor histories can preserve other effects of the past. Map removal is therefore not equivalent to erasing organizational experience or demonstrating organizational unlearning [R2, R13].

## 3. Why this task, and what has been adapted

The base task is repeated collective probabilistic judgment. Each round supplies a binary outcome, one context, and three binary source signals. The true outcome is independently drawn with probability .5. Contexts occur once each in a shuffled block of three. Conditional on the outcome and context, source correctness is independent. Reliability is stationary:

| Context | Source A | Source B | Source C |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| X | .85 | .65 | .50 |
| Y | .50 | .85 | .65 |
| Z | .65 | .50 | .85 |

The cyclic matrix makes source specialization contextual while equalizing average reliability at 2/3 across balanced contexts. There is no globally best source. The numbers create distinguishable channels with finite-sample ambiguity; they are **project design choices**, not empirical estimates from a field setting or parameters prescribed by the cited papers.

The organizational setting is deliberately abstract: a recurring decision group must judge cases from different contexts using role-labelled evidence whose reliability has to be learned. No additional occupational story is necessary for this identification task. A later field adaptation must establish what outcomes, source identities, feedback, and organizational records actually mean in that setting before attaching a familiar industry label.

| Source and established distinction | Assumption retained here | Project-specific adaptation and identification purpose | Boundary or omitted phenomenon |
|---|---|---|---|
| Huber separates distribution, interpretation, and memory [R1]. | Availability of facts and their use need not coincide. | Mechanically disclose all current signals before discussion; preserve the factual ledger across map conditions. Study interpretation after disclosure. | Does not test voluntary sharing, concealment, search, or discovery of who holds hidden facts. |
| Walsh and Ungson distinguish acquisition, retention, and retrieval [R2]. | A record and a retrieved representation are not interchangeable objects. | Store factual events separately from reliability estimates and manipulate only the declared map slot. | One external table is not the whole organization's memory. |
| March distinguishes individual beliefs and organizational code [R3]. | Collective agreement need not imply accuracy. | Measure the inherited map, individual estimates, and later forecasts separately. | Actors here receive direct truth feedback; this differs from March's learning model. |
| Bunderson separates expertise cues, attribution, and influence [R4]. | Attributed expertise may differ from task-relevant competence. | Known source reliability supplies an external criterion against which attribution can be measured. | No demographic status cues, hierarchy, prestige, or intrinsic model-ability treatment is present. |
| Brandon and Hollingshead distinguish task–expertise–person matching, accuracy, sharedness, and participation [R5]. | Expertise attribution should be context-specific and measured at several levels. | Use a context-by-source map and separate recall, own estimates, and behavioral responses. | Identical access is not shared belief; map consultation is not full TMS participation. |
| Hollenbeck and colleagues distinguish distributed evidence, member recommendations, and their combination [R6]. | Source evidence and actor advice are different levels. | Separate source signals, actor forecasts, and the mean team forecast. | Equal final weights do not fix actor advice quality or instantiate their hierarchical-team model. |
| Csaszar and Eggers compare aggregation structures with context-dependent expertise [R7]. | Structure and expertise matching can jointly affect judgment. | Fix equal averaging for the main within-Team map contrast; retain other architectures as bounded extensions. | The current study does not estimate the optimal organization or replicate their continuous project-quality model. |
| Haas and Hansen study knowledge use in its task context [R8]. | More available knowledge need not ensure better performance. | Keep the record available and ask whether its shared interpretation changes use. | Their consulting-field evidence does not establish that a redundant numeric map causes harm. |
| Anderson and Holt use controlled binary events and noisy signals [R9]. | A transparent probabilistic environment can distinguish available evidence from observed judgment. | Use known priors and source accuracy to calculate a researcher-side benchmark. | Their sequential public-decision cascade differs from synchronous exchange after full signal disclosure here. |
| Knudsen and Levinthal separate alternative generation and imperfect evaluation [R10]. | Evaluation is an organizational process that can be studied while holding opportunities fixed. | Pre-generate the exogenous event tape; compare judgments on the same cases. | There is no endogenous search, NK landscape, or generation of opportunities in this task. |
| Okhuysen and Eisenhardt study knowledge integration and formal interaction interventions [R11]. | Sharing and integration need not be the same process. | Fix disclosure, then vary representation content; later compare peer exchange with private memos. | Their experimental interventions do not validate the current message limit or synchronous protocol. |
| Lewis's measurement work and membership-change study distinguish TMS dimensions and inherited group knowledge [R12, R14]. | A single aggregate map score is insufficient to characterize group knowledge. | Record individual readouts, agreement, distortion, and later revision separately. | These are new synthetic measures, not a validated transfer of a human-team scale. No membership-change effect is claimed. |

Other simplifying choices have direct identification roles. Stationarity separates learning from environmental drift. Conditional independence avoids confounding a mistaken reliability estimate with unmodelled cue dependence. A fixed .5 IID prior prevents a known truth quota from generating an unmodelled history-dependent prior. A fixed message policy and mean aggregation reduce procedural variation. Each restriction removes an alternative explanation at the cost of scope. None makes the setting a representative sample of organizations.

The earlier truth-balanced macroblock design and the implemented IID-truth design must remain distinct in descriptions. The current prior is appropriate to the latter. Returning to fixed truth quotas would require a quota-aware benchmark under the information actually disclosed to actors.

## 4. The state fork and comparison hierarchy

At the checkpoint, preserve the permitted event ledger, behavioral records, and labelled actor histories. Future contexts, signals, and outcomes are matched across conditions. Only the assigned initial map and its declared provenance differ. Later forecasts, scores, messages, and updated maps are endogenous outcomes and need not remain identical.

The full-record case is the main scope. A records-truncated experiment answers a different question: what happens when the map becomes a privileged carrier of otherwise unavailable history? It is a useful later boundary test, not a required addition to the current check.

| Condition | Initial object | What its comparison can establish |
|---|---|---|
| E | Same-ledger Beta posterior estimates and uncertainty limits, displayed to the same precision as other numeric maps. | A reproducible interpretation based on observed evidence, not an oracle revealing true reliabilities. |
| P | A prespecified permutation of the complete cells of E. | P−E isolates the assigned distortion package under matched history, format, and provenance. |
| H | A map formed from independent actor readouts of the common history, combined under the declared rule. | H−E compares a naturally formed team representation with a mechanical evidence summary; it can include formation and provenance differences. |
| R0 | No inherited map object. | H−R0 combines availability, content, format, and possible reconstruction effects. Actors can still infer a map from records. |
| RU | Same-schema unknown-valued map. | RU−R0 distinguishes an explicit unknown representation from absence; it is not automatically zero or neutral. |
| R0 replicate | The same initial R0 state, executed again with new model requests. | A descriptive repeatability check at this checkpoint; one pair does not estimate a noise distribution. |

The primary causal contrast is P−E. The key substantive secondary is H−E. H−R0 and RU−R0 remain secondary with narrower interpretations. The main current architecture is the interactive Team. A no-peer-message condition preserves actors, disclosure, call stages, and aggregation while replacing peer-visible messages with private memos. Solo changes actor count and information path; nominal teams add independent full-information judgments and averaging. These are useful discriminations, not interchangeable controls or a reason to require every map-by-architecture cell before Stage 1.

Peer exchange remains an organizational mechanism to test, not a finding established by Team P−E. Both E and P are collectively accessible; their comparison does not manipulate whether participants know that others see the map. A common-knowledge or specifically social interpretation would need additional manipulations or evidence.

## 5. A reproducible planted distortion and eligible histories

Use **one distortion dose** for the current engineering check: within each context, swap the entire E cell associated with the truly best source with the cell associated with the truly worst source. Move estimate and uncertainty limits together. Keep the middle source unchanged. The researcher uses the true ordering only to construct treatment; actors never receive the true matrix, ordering, seed-selection statistic, or condition label.

E and P use the same schema, two-decimal display, ordering policy, and neutral provenance description. This is a distortion of a reliability-estimate-and-uncertainty package. It does not isolate point estimates from confidence intervals. A later point-estimate-only intervention would require an additional condition and a defensible treatment of intervals.

A finite history can make E cells tied or wrongly ordered. Consequently, swapping the nominally best and worst channels can leave the display unchanged or improve it. For a **selected engineering demonstration**, select histories offline before model calls using only the formation-period exogenous tape:

1. Compute E under the fixed prior and schema, then its displayed two-decimal values.
2. Construct P by the declared all-context permutation.
3. Require at least one displayed point estimate to change and require the nine-cell point-estimate mean squared error of displayed P against the true matrix to exceed that of displayed E.
4. Traverse a prespecified seed sequence and retain the first eligible history, or the first prespecified number of eligible histories. Record the full selection rule, examined seeds, eligibility measures, and retained seed before actor responses.
5. Do not condition selection on H formation, actor forecasts, P−E outcomes, or the favorable sign of a future-tape loss difference.

The eligibility rule defines an intentionally selected diagnostic case. It does not estimate the natural frequency of misleading histories. Increased matrix error also does **not** guarantee increased realized cBR: the next signal vector may be uninformative about the swapped distinction, and actors may disregard or correct the map. A null behavioral effect is therefore possible even with an eligible treatment.

For a later confirmatory P study, choose explicitly between all generated histories and this formation-based eligible population. The recommendation is to retain the eligible-population estimand for a sharply defined distortion intervention, report its selection rate, and keep the natural-H study on a separate representative or explicitly stratified history sample. Do not silently apply distortion eligibility to estimates of natural H formation.

## 6. Formation, exposure, and revision

H formation uses actual actor responses to the permitted history. Preserve each response before computing the combined map; do not retain only the median. Coordinate-wise median aggregation is an implementation choice that controls one source of idiosyncrasy. It is not a literature-derived model of negotiation or proof of emergent consensus.

The revised engineering design equalizes the number of readouts for each updated map object at three: Team and no-peer-message use one readout from each of their three actors; Solo uses three independent readouts from clones of its own checkpoint; each nominal member uses three readouts from clones of that member's own checkpoint. Nominal members' readouts are not pooled across peers. These choices equalize readout count, not actor identity, computational history, diversity, or the effect of aggregation. All architecture comparisons in the short run remain descriptive.

A neutral provenance line should truthfully identify the object as an inherited summary available to all teammates. E and P must use identical provenance wording so their content contrast does not become an authority manipulation. H may truthfully be identified as actor-formed; this difference must remain part of the interpretation of H−E. Do not falsely call E or P a team-endorsed belief.

For the later organizational study, the recommended lifecycle is **initialized and subsequently revisable block memory**. Retain the assigned object until the first scheduled update. After that, updates follow the same rule across conditions. This asks whether an initially inherited interpretation affects a later learning trajectory. It does not mean the object is displayed only once.

Keep the inherited checkpoint object as an immutable audit reference, not as an additional live input after replacement. At an update checkpoint, append truth feedback, take the read-only measurement snapshot, and only then perform the live update. A standing-map condition instead fixes the assigned map throughout; it tests sustained exposure and cannot demonstrate resistance to an allowed update.

The established candidate of 24 formation rounds, 84 future rounds, and updates every 12 future rounds remains a transparent basis for planning. This document does not describe those quantities as power-validated. A study intended to assess correction must include decisions after an actual update. An update after the final observed decision only verifies the update path.

## 7. Measurement: distinguish delivery, endorsement, use, and consequence

| Layer | Planned measurement | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Treatment delivery | Reconstruct the condition from the permitted checkpoint and compare expected map content, provenance, precision, records, and isolation with the exact actor request. Include deliberately wrong-map and missing-map rejection tests. | A valid hash alone does not prove the correct treatment was delivered. |
| Display recall | Ask what the supplied map states, using a read-only checkpoint clone. R0 has no supplied numeric object to recall. | Accurate copying does not establish belief. |
| Own reliability estimate | Ask separately for the actor's own estimates from a fresh clone of the same checkpoint, without preceding it with a recall answer. | A prompted report is not direct access to an internal mental state. |
| Sharedness | Dispersion of individual own-estimate readouts before aggregation, and pairwise agreement, reported separately from accuracy. | Three identical supplied tables do not establish shared endorsement. |
| Representation accuracy | Nine-cell mean squared error and cellwise signed errors relative to the true matrix; also distance from the same-evidence E benchmark. | Truth accuracy and conformity to finite evidence answer different questions. |
| Behavioral use | No-feedback shadow profiles varying source signals from otherwise identical cloned states. | A fitted coefficient is not a process explanation unless the model is recoverable and fits adequately. |
| Judgment | Aggregate probabilistic forecast, conditional Brier regret, and realized Brier score. | A loss difference does not identify its unique cognitive pathway. |

The future shadow-probe recommendation is all eight three-source signal profiles in each of three contexts, each from a fresh unchanged snapshot. This supports contrasts in response to source signals. A batch of profiles in one request is a different measurement because answers may depend on the other profiles. Probes must not append feedback or mutate the live state. The current call-limited check need not include these probes and cannot claim their validation.

An additive logit model is a candidate behavioral summary, not the definition of evidence use. Specify intercept, probability clipping for that analysis, interactions, and residual checks before fitting it. A direct finite difference in forecast response is a less assumption-heavy descriptive alternative. Distinguish relative source use from overall response strength and probability calibration. Do not infer w or ledger-reading parameter lambda from improved performance alone.

The formation link is a separate analysis. Compute the exogenous history statistic M, the count of strictly reversed source pairs, and actual H error before the fork. Examine whether the history statistics predict the generated map, while retaining failed links as results. A positive P contrast cannot substitute for this step. Selecting histories by their realized H distortion would change the natural-H target population and must not be introduced after responses are seen.

## 8. Pre-analysis commitments for a later adequately sized study

These commitments concern a future study, not the current small engineering run. The final manifest must lock model, prompts, selection rule, horizons, update mode, sample size, order schedule, and analysis code before confirmatory outcomes are inspected.

### 8.1 Unit, target, and outcome

The independent sampling unit is a **formation history / fork family**. Rounds, actors, map cells, and probes are repeated measurements inside that family. Repeated model executions on the same tape are a second sampling level, not new independent histories.

Let p(f,a,r,t) be the final aggregate probability for history f, condition a, response replicate r, and future round t. Let q(f,t) be the researcher-side posterior using the true reliability matrix and full current signal vector. Define cBR = (p−q)^2. It equals the conditional expected Brier excess loss; it is not the realized round-by-round difference in Brier scores. The probability q is never supplied to actors.

For each window W, compute the within-family difference:

```text
d_f(W) = mean over paired replicates and t in W [cBR(P) − cBR(E)]
Delta(W) = mean of d_f(W) across independently sampled eligible histories
```

Average replicates within a history before estimating uncertainty across histories, or use a prespecified hierarchical analysis that preserves this nesting. Positive Delta means worse judgment under the assigned distortion; neither eligibility nor theory forces its sign.

### 8.2 Windows and multiplicity

For U=12, the prespecified windows are pre-update rounds 1–12, post-update rounds 13–T, and the full horizon 1–T. The first two are disjoint; the full horizon overlaps both. For the 84-round candidate, T=84. Report P−E for both pre-update and full horizon as required scientific contrasts; report post-update separately to locate persistence or correction.

If pre-update and full-horizon results are both treated as confirmatory, define them as one two-comparison family and use Holm adjustment for their two-sided tests. Report estimates and interval uncertainty even when neither rejects zero. Do not choose the more favorable window after seeing trajectories. Post-update and natural-H moderation remain secondary unless separately powered and preregistered.

A fixed standing map has no realized update boundary. A one-future-round check has no post-update outcome window. Do not manufacture separate evidence by attaching the names of future windows to the same observations.

### 8.3 Secondary analyses and moderators

Estimate H−E on the prespecified natural-history sample, with strict pre-fork reversal count as the principal history moderator. Include M alongside it rather than silently redefining the historical M variable. Distinguish a ledger-derived reversal from reversal in H. Report H error and individual disagreement as separate pretreatment measurements.

Treat H−R0, RU−R0, and architecture differences as secondary. If no-peer-message is added, compare the P−E or H−E contrast between architectures rather than attributing a raw Team–Solo gap to interaction. Such an interaction still reflects the declared exchange protocol; it does not uniquely identify social endorsement, common knowledge, or authority.

Do not estimate causal mediation from correlations among map error, reported belief, and loss. These may be useful process evidence, but post-treatment variables can be jointly affected by the intervention.

### 8.4 Response variability, scheduling, and A/A

The small check includes one independently requested R0 replicate from the same checkpoint. Report its signed and absolute difference and the exact number of observations. Call it a repeatability observation, not a noise floor estimate, equivalence test, or distribution of model randomness.

Before a formal study, use a separate measurement pilot with repeated requests on several independently generated histories to estimate within-history response variability and between-history variation. Balance branch order across histories and interleave matched conditions where supported. Use the same scheduling policy for P, E, H, and repeats. Record actual serving identity when available, configured alias, timestamps, transport, prompt hashes, and unresolved host-context limitations. A requested seed is not proof of deterministic model sampling.

Rotate source-list and message order and record role assignments. This controls the positions actually rotated; it does not guarantee that every serialized JSON field or map-cell position is balanced. Any additional position manipulation must be declared rather than claimed retrospectively.

### 8.5 Sample-size decision and failure rules

Do not inherit N=180, a surrogate power calculation, or a three-seed diagnostic as justification for a confirmatory sample. Before that study, choose a smallest scientifically meaningful cBR difference, estimate the nested variance from an independent pilot, and simulate the declared matched design and analysis across a prespecified sample-size grid. Examine sensitivity to conservative variance assumptions and model-version heterogeneity. Lock the smallest affordable design that meets the declared precision or power criterion; if none does, report a development study rather than a confirmatory test. The small current run provides no defensible variance estimate for this calculation.

Archive every attempted model request, failure, and restart. Prespecify whether a failed request is retried, and include all attempts in the call budget. Do not silently replace invalid forecasts, carry forward estimates, drop unfavorable histories, or rerun until significance appears. Report missing matched outcomes by condition and cause. A complete-pair estimate is conditional on completion; accompany it with a sensitivity analysis or worst-case bounds using the bounded outcome when failures could depend on treatment. Partial traces remain valuable engineering evidence but are not completed experimental units.

## 9. Rival explanations and tests that could weaken the claim

| Rival explanation | Discriminating evidence or falsification | Consequence for interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| A table changes attention regardless of its contents. | E and P have matched schema, precision, provenance, and exposure; later include a content-preserving display control if necessary. | P−E narrows this rival but does not remove every salience difference created by the numbers. |
| Actors simply copy displayed numbers. | Separate recall from own estimates; inspect forecast responses on conflicting-signal shadow profiles. | Recall without belief or behavioral change is successful delivery, not inherited evidence use. |
| Records are available but not processed. | Compare own estimates with same-evidence E and inspect behavior; later vary record accessibility explicitly. | Full-record availability does not establish faithful reading. P−E cannot by itself separate all reading strategies. |
| Early history never distorted the actual map. | Independently test history statistics against H error and disagreement. | A missing formation link weakens the endogenous REM claim even if planted distortion affects behavior. |
| Differences reflect backend drift or response variability. | Interleaving, timestamp/model records, and genuinely repeated A/A runs. | One repeat cannot rule out drift or quantify a stable noise floor. |
| The effect is individual response to a prompt, not collective interpretation. | Add matched no-peer-message and, subsequently, common-access/provenance manipulations. | Team-only evidence supports a shared-object intervention in a team workflow, not a uniquely social mechanism. |
| Error persists only because revision is prohibited. | In the revisable design, observe decisions after scheduled updates and measure actual revisions. | Standing exposure is informative but does not establish failed correction after an opportunity to update. |
| A score improvement reflects calibration rather than relative cue use. | Report source-response contrasts separately from overall probability extremity and loss. | Do not equate every loss change with better expertise attribution. |
| P improves an already mistaken finite-sample E. | Formation-only eligibility checks and recorded cellwise distortion. | Ineligible histories cannot be relabelled successful planted-error demonstrations after observing outcomes. |
| A software check passes while the wrong map is delivered. | Reconstruct expected treatment semantics and reject deliberately corrupted maps, provenance, or state. | Structural validity and replay are necessary but insufficient for experimental validity. |

Several null patterns are substantively informative. Correct recall with unchanged own estimates and forecasts suggests resistance to the intervention at that checkpoint. Changed own estimates without changed forecasts suggests a report–behavior separation. Changed forecasts without worse cBR indicates that map use and harm are distinct. None should trigger undisclosed tuning of the stimulus until a desired result appears.

For assurance research, the corresponding workflow is **monitor → audit → revalidate → experimentally replace or reset**. Monitoring records predictions and feedback; auditing separates the supplied object from own estimates; revalidation compares representations with the available-evidence benchmark and subsequent behavioral responses. For now, use scheduled research checkpoints rather than an unvalidated automated reset threshold: fork and each declared update boundary in the longer candidate. Replacement remains an assigned experimental condition, not a recommendation to reset a deployed system whenever a particular score is crossed. A future adaptive reset policy would need a separate prespecified trigger, costs, comparison policy, and prospective validation. This makes the assurance lifecycle testable without presenting it as an already validated governance solution.

## 10. Current scope and the next scientific boundaries

The present authorization is **at most 20 model calls per branch**, using `gpt-5.6-luna`. This configured model name is not proof of an immutable served version. The planned revised configuration has three formation rounds and one future round, with initialized-update mode and U=1. The update occurs after the sole future decision, so there is no observed decision after that update. The first eligible integer seed from 20261025 onward is selected under the formation-only rule in section 5.

The planned branches are Team P/E/H/R0/R0-replicate/RU, Solo H/R0, no-peer-message H/R0, and nominal H/R0. Split fork audits are included for Team, Solo, and no-peer-message. Nominal fork audits are omitted from this live configuration to respect the cap; testing them offline is not equivalent to observing their live validity. The shared formation sequence requires 30 model calls. Each branch is planned to require at most 18 calls, and the declared successful-path total is 219 calls. These are planned counts, not a claim of completion; the executable configuration and actual attempt ledger govern the final accounting.

Count formation/readout, audit, update, repeat, failure, and retry calls explicitly wherever they occur; shared setup is not free and must remain visible in the total. The short sequence deliberately departs from 24+84. It checks assigned maps, independent audit questions, branch isolation, scoring, repeat execution, and an update path. It supplies no estimate of a population treatment effect, correction speed, or formal power. The single-future-round P−E and R0-repeat differences are illustrations on one selected history, not evidence about average organizational behavior.

The next scientific sequence should be driven by evidence:

1. **Treatment and measurement validity:** demonstrate correct map delivery; separate recall and own estimates; test semantic rejection of wrong conditions; characterize repeated responses on multiple histories.
2. **Endogenous formation:** establish whether the prescribed formation process produces measurable, reproducible H error related to exogenous history variation, without selecting favorable realized H responses.
3. **Content and correction:** estimate P−E and H−E over enough independent histories, including decisions after updates.
4. **Interaction boundary:** test whether matched no-peer-message changes the content effect before interpreting it as specifically collective.
5. **Information boundary:** contrast full records with explicit record truncation; keep deletion of facts separate from replacement of interpretations.
6. **Environmental and organizational boundaries:** vary cue dependence, reliability drift, unequal expertise, aggregation, and actor heterogeneity one at a time with new benchmarks and predictions. Human or field adaptation requires independent construct validation.

The reusable contribution is a way to make those organizational questions experimentally distinguishable. Better model scores, faster prompting, or a larger collection of runs are not the central scientific objective.
